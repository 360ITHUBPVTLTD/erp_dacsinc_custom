Accounts Guide

First learn two things: what debit and credit are, and the types of entries in ERPNext. Then use the How to part for your daily work.

1Debit and credit — in 2 minutes

Every entry in accounts has two sides: a Debit (Dr) side and a Credit (Cr) side, always with the same amount.

Remember just one rule:
Debit = where the money (or goods) goes  ·  Credit = where it comes from

Examples

For accountants: the three golden rules
  • People (customers, suppliers): debit the receiver, credit the giver.
  • Things (bank, cash, stock): debit what comes in, credit what goes out.
  • Expenses / income: debit expenses and losses, credit income and gains.

2Types of entries in ERPNext

In ERPNext each kind of event has its own document. You fill the document and click Submit, and ERPNext writes the debit and credit for you. Here are the ones the accounts team uses:

🧾Sales Invoice

We bill a customer for goods sold.

Example: Sold uniforms ₹10,000 + GST ₹1,800
Customer (Debtors)Dr 11,800
SalesCr 10,000
Output GSTCr 1,800
The customer now owes us.
📥Purchase Invoice

We enter a supplier's bill.

Example: Fabric ₹5,000 + GST ₹900
Stock (goods bought)Dr 5,000
Input GSTDr 900
Supplier (Creditors)Cr 5,900
We now owe the supplier.
💰Payment Entry — Receive

A customer pays us.

Example: Customer pays ₹11,800 by NEFT
BankDr 11,800
Customer (Debtors)Cr 11,800
Money into the bank; the customer owes nothing now.
💸Payment Entry — Pay

We pay a supplier or jobber.

Example: We pay the supplier ₹5,900
Supplier (Creditors)Dr 5,900
BankCr 5,900
Money out of the bank; we owe nothing now.
🔁Payment Entry — Internal Transfer

Money moves between our own accounts.

Example: Cash ₹20,000 deposited in the bank
BankDr 20,000
CashCr 20,000
Only our own money moved.
↩️Credit Note

A customer returns goods (the sale in reverse).

Example: Return of ₹1,000 + GST ₹180
SalesDr 1,000
Output GSTDr 180
Customer (Debtors)Cr 1,180
The customer owes us less. The goods come back with a Sales Return (from the Delivery Note).
↪️Debit Note

We return goods to a supplier (the bill in reverse).

Example: Return of ₹2,000 + GST ₹360
Supplier (Creditors)Dr 2,360
Stock / purchaseCr 2,000
Input GSTCr 360
We owe the supplier less. The goods go out with a Purchase Return (from the Purchase Receipt).
📝Journal Entry

Anything with no other document: bank charges, TDS deposit, corrections. You type both sides yourself.

Example: Bank charges ₹50 on the statement
Bank Charges (expense)Dr 50
BankCr 50
Use only when no other document fits.
🚚Delivery Note

Goods leave our warehouse for a customer.

Example: Goods that cost us ₹7,000
Cost of Goods SoldDr 7,000
Stock In HandCr 7,000
Writes the stock side of a sale.
📦Purchase Receipt

Goods from a supplier arrive.

Example: Goods worth ₹5,000 received
Stock In HandDr 5,000
Stock Received But Not BilledCr 5,000
The bill (Purchase Invoice) clears the "not billed" part.
No accounts entry: Sales Order, Purchase Order, Material Request and Pick List only plan the work. They write no debit or credit.

Good to know

  • Draft = saved, but nothing written to the accounts yet. Submit = the debit and credit are written.
  • Cancel = ERPNext writes the same lines on the opposite sides, so it is as if it never happened. Then Amend to make a corrected copy.
  • To see what a document wrote: on an invoice, Delivery Note or Purchase Receipt click View › Accounting Ledger; on a Payment Entry or Journal Entry click the Ledger button. For one customer, supplier or account: report General Ledger. (Cancelled entries are hidden there unless you tick Show Cancelled Entries.)
  • On a Payment Entry, Paid To is the debit side (where the money goes) and Paid From is the credit side (where it comes from).
  • On a Journal Entry, you type the Debit and Credit columns yourself. The two totals must be equal.

🧾 Submit a sales invoice

After goods are delivered, the sales invoice is made from the Delivery Note and waits as a draft. A draft is not a bill yet: the customer owes nothing until you submit it.

  1. Go to Order Flow › Finance › Receivables and click the Draft pill.
  2. Click the arrow next to an invoice to see its items. Check the customer, items, rate, GST and total.
  3. Click Submit on the row, read the summary, tick the box and confirm.
In the accounts: the customer now owes the invoice total (₹11,800 for ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST). Our sales go up by ₹10,000, and ₹1,800 GST is due to the government.

ERPNext writes (example figures):

Sales Invoice ₹10,000 + GST 18% (same state)
AccountDebitCredit
Debtors - IND (the customer)11,800.00
Sales - IND10,000.00
Output Tax CGST - IND900.00
Output Tax SGST - IND900.00

The Delivery Note before it already wrote the stock side: the cost of the goods left the warehouse.

Delivery Note goods that cost us ₹7,000 left the warehouse
AccountDebitCredit
Cost of Goods Sold - IND7,000.00
Stock In Hand - IND7,000.00
Something wrong? Don't submit it. Open the invoice, correct it and save, or ask whoever made it.

💰 Receive money from a customer

  1. Go to Order Flow › Finance › Receivables. Find the invoice with the search box or the Customer filter.
  2. Click Receive on that invoice. (On the invoice itself, the same button is Receive Payment.)
  3. Amount settled on this invoice: how much of the invoice this payment clears. Paid in full? Click Full outstanding.
  4. Mode of Payment: NEFT, Cheque, Cash, UPI… For a bank payment, type the UTR / cheque number and its date.
  5. Bank cut charges, or the customer cut TDS? Open ③ Bank charges, TDS, write-off and type them in.
  6. Check the box on the right: "Into bank" must match your bank statement.
  7. Click Review, read it once, tick the box, click Submit Payment.

📥 Enter a supplier's bill

First the store enters the goods that arrived (Purchase Receipt). Then you enter the supplier's bill from that receipt, so the quantities and rates match.

  1. Go to Order Flow › Purchase Flow › To Bill. It lists the receipts still waiting for a bill.
  2. Open the receipt and click Create › Purchase Invoice.
    Or: Accounting › Purchase Invoice › New › choose the supplier › Get Items from PR.
  3. Type the Supplier Invoice No and Supplier Invoice Date exactly as on their bill.
  4. Check the quantity, rate, GST and total against the paper bill. Different? Stop and check with purchase.
  5. Save, then Submit.
In the accounts: we now owe the supplier the bill total. The GST on the bill is ours to claim back.

ERPNext writes (example figures):

Purchase Receipt (by the store) goods worth ₹50,000 arrived
AccountDebitCredit
Stock In Hand - IND50,000.00
Stock Received But Not Billed - IND50,000.00
Purchase Invoice (by you) bill ₹50,000 + GST ₹9,000
AccountDebitCredit
Stock Received But Not Billed - IND50,000.00
Input Tax CGST - IND4,500.00
Input Tax SGST - IND4,500.00
Creditors - IND (the supplier)59,000.00

"Stock Received But Not Billed" is a waiting account: the receipt puts ₹50,000 on its credit side, and the bill takes it off on the debit side, back to zero.

Jobber's bill? Same steps. It then appears under Jobber Payables on the Finance tab.

💸 Pay a supplier or jobber

  1. Go to Order Flow › Finance › Supplier Payables (or Jobber Payables) and find the bill.
  2. Click Pay. (On the bill itself, the button is Make Payment.)
  3. Amount settled on this bill: how much of the bill this payment clears (Full outstanding for all of it).
  4. Choose the Mode of Payment, then type the UTR / cheque number and date.
  5. Our bank took charges? Type them in Bank charges. TDS from the supplier: see the note below.
  6. Review › tick › Submit Payment.
What happened (bill ₹59,000)SettledDeductionsSupplier getsOut of our bank
Paid in full59,000—59,00059,000
We cut ₹1,000 TDS59,000TDS 1,00058,00058,000
Our bank took ₹15 charges59,000Bank charges 1559,00059,015
TDS on suppliers — the ERPNext way: set a Tax Withholding Category (e.g. 194C contract) on the Supplier. ERPNext then deducts the TDS on the Purchase Invoice itself (Cr TDS account, and we owe the supplier that much less), and you pay only the balance. The TDS box in the Pay prompt is only for TDS that is not already on the bill. Our suppliers don't have a category yet, so ask the accounts head which to use.
In the accounts: we owe the supplier less, and our bank goes down. TDS we cut is owed to the government: deposit it monthly (see Deposit TDS).

⏩ Money came before the invoice (advance)

  1. Accounting › Payment Entry › New.
  2. Payment Type: Receive · Party Type: Customer · Party: the customer.
  3. Mode of Payment, then Paid Amount. Type the reference number and date.
  4. Leave the invoice table empty. Save › Submit.
  5. When the invoice is made later, open it and use Get Advances Received to use up the advance (for a supplier advance, the Purchase Invoice has Get Advances Paid). Or: Accounting › Payment Reconciliation › choose the customer › match the advance with the invoice.
In the accounts: our bank goes up, and the customer has money "kept" with us until the invoice comes.

ERPNext writes (example figures):

Advance received ₹25,000 before any invoice
AccountDebitCredit
Bank (YES BANK)25,000.00
Debtors - IND (the customer — shown as advance)25,000.00

Paying a supplier in advance works the same way, with Payment Type: Pay and Party Type: Supplier.

📑 One cheque for many customers / invoices

Example: cheque 123456 for ₹50,000 pays three invoices of two customers:

CustomerInvoiceAmount
Customer AINV-00110,000
Customer AINV-00215,000
Customer BINV-01025,000
Cheque total50,000
Remember: one Payment Entry can have only one customer. So one cheque for two customers needs two Payment Entries (option 1, the one we use), or one Journal Entry (option 2).

Option 1 (use this) — one Payment Entry per customer, same cheque number

For each customer on the cheque:

  1. Accounting › Payment Entry › New. Payment Type: Receive · Party Type: Customer · Party: the customer.
  2. Mode of Payment: Cheque. Paid Amount = this customer's share (A: 25,000 · B: 25,000).
  3. Cheque / Reference No: 123456 and the cheque date. Use the same number on every entry, so all parts of the cheque can be found together.
  4. Click Get Outstanding Invoices, tick this customer's invoices, and check the Allocated amount on each (A: INV-001 10,000 + INV-002 15,000).
  5. Remarks: "Cheque 123456 ₹50,000 — also for Customer B". Save › Submit.

When the cheque clears — Bank Reconciliation Tool

First import the bank statement (Accounting › Bank Statement Import), so ERPNext has the bank's lines. The statement shows one credit of ₹50,000. In Accounting › Bank Reconciliation Tool, choose the bank account and dates, click that bank line, and tick both Payment Entries:

Bank statement line16-10-2026 · CHQ 123456 · Cr ₹50,000
matched to
Payment Entry ACC-PAY-…-001 · Customer A · ₹25,000
Payment Entry ACC-PAY-…-002 · Customer B · ₹25,000
25,000 + 25,000 = 50,000 ✓

One customer, many invoices, one cheque? It's the same, with just one Payment Entry: Get Outstanding Invoices and tick all their invoices. (The Receive button on the Finance tab works one invoice at a time, so for this case use the Payment Entry as above.)

Option 2 — one Journal Entry for the whole cheque

Use this only if you want one voucher for the cheque.

  1. Accounting › Journal Entry › New. Entry Type: Bank Entry · Reference Number: 123456 and Reference Date = the cheque date.
  2. Row 1: the bank → Debit 50,000.
  3. One row per invoice: Debtors - IND, Party Type: Customer, Party, Reference Type: Sales Invoice, Reference Name: the invoice → Credit its amount.
  4. Check Total Debit = Total Credit (50,000). Save › Submit.

Don't know the split yet?

  1. Make one Payment Entry per customer with their amount, but tick no invoices. The money sits as an advance on the customer.
  2. When you know the split: Accounting › Payment Reconciliation › choose the customer › Get Unreconciled Entries › match the payment to the invoices › Reconcile.

⛔ A cheque bounced

  1. Find every entry for that cheque: in the Payment Entry list, filter Cheque/Reference No = the cheque number (Journal Entry list: Reference Number).
  2. Open each one › Cancel. ERPNext reverses the bank and customer lines, and the invoices go back to Unpaid / Overdue.
  3. Bounce charges from the bank: a Journal Entry (Bank Entry) with Bank Charges - IND in Debit and the bank in Credit.
  4. Charging those to the customer? Add them to the customer's next invoice, or ask the accounts head.
  5. When the customer pays again, receive it as normal with the new reference.

↩️ Customer returned goods (Sales Return + Credit Note)

A return has two parts in ERPNext: the goods come back into the warehouse, and the money the customer owes goes down. Do both:

  1. Goods back: open the Delivery Note for those goods › Create › Sales Return. Keep only the items and quantities actually returned, choose the warehouse they come back to, then Save › Submit.
  2. Money side: open the customer's Sales Invoice › Create › Return / Credit Note. Keep only the same items and quantities, then Save › Submit.
  3. If the customer had already paid, the credit note stays as a credit on the customer. It is used against their next invoice, or refunded with a Payment Entry (Payment Type: Pay).
In the accounts: the stock and its cost come back, the customer owes us less, and our sales and GST payable go down.

ERPNext writes (goods sold for ₹1,000 + GST ₹180 that cost us ₹700):

Sales Return (from the Delivery Note) goods come back
AccountDebitCredit
Stock In Hand - IND700.00
Cost of Goods Sold - IND700.00
Credit Note (from the Sales Invoice) the sale reversed
AccountDebitCredit
Sales - IND1,000.00
Output Tax CGST - IND90.00
Output Tax SGST - IND90.00
Debtors - IND (the customer)1,180.00

A Credit Note made without the Sales Return changes only the money. The stock in ERPNext would stay wrong.

↪️ We returned goods to a supplier (Purchase Return + Debit Note)

  1. Goods out: open the Purchase Receipt for those goods › Create › Purchase Return. Keep only the items and quantities returned, then Save › Submit.
  2. Money side: open the supplier's Purchase Invoice › Create › Return / Debit Note. Keep the same items and quantities, type the supplier's credit note number if they gave one, then Save › Submit.
In the accounts: the stock goes down, we owe the supplier less, and the GST we claim on those goods goes down.

🏦 Bank charges or interest on the bank statement

Use this only for amounts not linked to any invoice, such as monthly bank charges, SMS charges or interest.

  1. Accounting › Journal Entry › New. Entry Type: Bank Entry. Posting date = the date on the statement.
  2. Row 1: Bank Charges - IND → amount in Debit.
  3. Row 2: your bank account → the same amount in Credit.
  4. Type the statement reference and a User Remark (e.g. "SMS charges, Sept"). Save › Submit.
Example 1 — the bank statement shows ₹354 charges Entry Type: Bank Entry
AccountDebitCredit
Bank Charges - IND354.00
Bank (YES BANK)354.00

For interest received, swap the sides: Bank in Debit, and an interest income account in Credit.

🏛️ Deposit TDS with the government

The TDS we cut from suppliers has to be paid to the government by the 7th of the next month (30 April for March).

  1. Pay the TDS on the government portal and keep the challan.
  2. Accounting › Journal Entry › New, Entry Type: Bank Entry.
  3. Row 1: the TDS account (e.g. TDS on Contract - IND) → amount in Debit.
  4. Row 2: your bank account → the same amount in Credit.
  5. Reference = challan number and date. Save › Submit.

✏️ Correct a mistake

MistakeWhat to do
Wrong amount, date or mode on a submitted paymentOpen the Payment Entry › Cancel › Amend › correct it › Submit.
Wrong item, rate or GST on a submitted invoiceCancel its payments first, then the invoice › Amend › correct › Submit. Then receive / pay again.
Anything on a draftJust open it, correct it and save. A draft has not touched the accounts yet.
An expense booked to the wrong accountJournal Entry: put the correct account in Debit and the wrong account in Credit (example below).
Example 3 — a ₹2,000 courier bill was booked to Sales Promotion by mistake Entry Type: Journal Entry

Stuck? Do this

What you seeDo this

No Receive / Pay buttonSubmit the invoice first. Already paid in full? Nothing to receive. Still missing? Ask the admin for Payment Entry rights.
"Into bank" doesn't match the bank statementCheck the TDS and bank charges you typed. The bank amount must equal the statement.
"Cannot cancel" an invoiceCancel its payment(s) first, then the invoice.
"Difference Amount" on a Payment EntryCorrect the TDS / bank charges / write-off until the difference is 0.
Journal Entry won't submitTotal Debit must equal total Credit. Customer / supplier lines need the Party.
